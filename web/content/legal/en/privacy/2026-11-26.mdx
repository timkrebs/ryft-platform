## 2. What RYFT is

RYFT is an iPhone app. With your permission it reads data from Apple Health, sends it to the RYFT servers and computes recovery, strain, sleep and readiness from it. If you turn on saving workouts, it also writes your completed strength workouts to Apple Health (the type, start and end, the name RYFT and RYFT's identifier of the session; no heart rate and no energy). It is currently a free beta, distributed through TestFlight to invited testers only.

Some parts described below are not yet available to you, or only to a small group of testers: the food diary (Section 3), the morning check-in (Section 3) and direct connections to Garmin and Suunto (Section 4). This policy says how they work when they arrive. Until a part is available to you, RYFT processes none of the data it describes.

- **Morning check-in, where it is available to you** (not yet in the app): when you went to bed and woke up, as you report them, with your time zone; how well you think you slept and how fresh you feel, each from 1 to 10; whether you feel sore; and, for a workout, how hard it felt, from 1 to 10. ## 4. Direct connections to Garmin and Suunto (not yet available)

Today, data from a Garmin or Suunto device reaches RYFT only through Apple Health (Section 3). RYFT is preparing direct connections: at your request, RYFT links your RYFT account to your Garmin or Suunto account, and that provider then delivers your data to RYFT directly. Nothing is connected unless you start it in the app, sign in at the provider and agree there; before that, the app shows what RYFT will read.

- **What RYFT would receive, and why:** your sleep (its stages and times) and overnight heart rate variability, which Apple Health does not carry from these devices, to compute your recovery, sleep and readiness with them. Only the kinds of data you allow at the provider; RYFT reads what the provider reports you allowed. Receiving further data from a provider (such as activities for coaching) needs a new version of this policy first.
- **What RYFT keeps about the connection:** the provider's identifier for your account, the permissions you granted, when you connected and when data last arrived, and the provider's access tokens, encrypted with a key that only the RYFT service handling connections can read. Each delivery is kept as received until it has been processed, then emptied; a delivery that cannot be processed is deleted after 14 days at the latest, and the record that it arrived (its kind, the provider's identifier of the record, a checksum and the times, without the content) after 30 days. The data itself is stored and used like your health data from Apple Health.
- **Disconnecting:** you can disconnect at any time in the app, or in your Garmin or Suunto account. Disconnecting in the app deletes everything the connection delivered and RYFT asks the provider to end RYFT's access; if the provider cannot be reached, RYFT retries for up to 30 days, keeping only what that needs (the encrypted tokens, your account identifier and the provider's identifier for your account), and then gives up. If you end the connection at the provider, RYFT deletes what it received in the same way. ## 7. Usage analytics (only if you say yes)

If you agree, RYFT sends pseudonymous usage events to PostHog, a product analytics service run by PostHog Inc., which processes them on our behalf under a data processing agreement. The controller is the one named in Section 1. We use these events to see which parts of the app people use and where they get stuck, so we can improve RYFT. We don't use them for advertising, and we don't link them to your account.

**Your consent.** Nothing is sent or stored for analytics until you agree. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; for storing and reading the ID on your phone, § 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw it at any time in Profile → Privacy → Share usage analytics. Sending stops at once, and events not yet sent are deleted from your phone. Withdrawing doesn't affect the lawfulness of what was sent before.

**What is sent.** A random ID that RYFT creates for this installation. It isn't your account, email, name or Apple ID. It's deleted when you turn analytics off or sign out, and you get a new one if you turn analytics on again. With it, these events:

- which onboarding step you're on, which screen you opened, and that you asked to connect Apple Health (not what you allowed);
- that the first sync finished;
- which features you used, for example that you saved a journal entry or started a training session;
- that an error was shown, by its type or error code (for example "offline", "500" or "consent_required");
- for food logging: whether a search found anything, a range for how many characters it had, how many results it had and how long the results took; how high up the list the food you opened was, whether it's a generic or a branded food, and whether it had a default or last-used portion; when you save a meal, how you added its foods (quick add, the food's page, a barcode, a suggestion or a private food), whether in portions (catalogue or last-used), grams or millilitres, and whether a portion count was fractional, but not how many foods; and that you reopened a saved meal;
- for the sync button on the Home screen and Profile → Sync now: that you tapped it, whether it worked, and a range for how long it took;
- when the app was installed, opened or closed, with the app version and build, iOS version, device type, the analytics library's version and a session ID.

**What is never sent.** Your health data, or anything read from Apple Health or calculated from it (sleep, heart rate, HRV, recovery, strain, weight, scores). What you type or search for, food names, amounts, calories or nutrients. Your name, email or account ID. No screen recordings, and nothing about your taps beyond the events listed here. We don't share the events with anyone besides PostHog and its sub-processors.

**Where.** The events are stored on PostHog's servers in the EU (eu.i.posthog.com). PostHog Inc. is a US company. Where it accesses the data from the US, this is covered by the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR) in our data processing agreement with PostHog. When the app connects, PostHog receives your IP address. We have set PostHog to discard it and not to derive a location from it.

**Retention.** Until they are sent, up to 200 events wait on your phone. PostHog keeps the events for 12 months and then deletes them.

**Your rights.** You have the rights to access, erasure, restriction and objection, as set out in Section 9. Because the ID is random, not linked to your account and not shown in the app, we cannot find your events. Once it's deleted (on opt-out or sign-out), we can no longer tell which events were yours (Art. 11 GDPR).

## 10. Security

All connections are encrypted (TLS). Each RYFT service runs under its own account, has its own database role and credentials and can reach only what it needs; keys and passwords are kept in Google's Secret Manager, in Belgium. The Sign in with Apple token and the providers' tokens are encrypted with a key that only the service that needs them can read. Data is stored encrypted at Supabase and Google. A firewall (Cloud Armor) protects the servers, and the device check above helps protect them as it is introduced. Only the operator has administrative access: to the admin tools (a sign-in with a second factor, or a key kept in Secret Manager), with every administrative request recorded, and to the database and the cloud projects. The admin tools show the operator account details (including email addresses, sign-in methods and the last sign-in, time zone and maximum heart rate), devices, data sources, consent and feedback, and summaries of how complete your data is (for example the number of days scored), but no scores or health values, so that the operator can help you and fix problems.

## 11. Age

You must be at least 16 years old to use RYFT.

## 12. No automated decisions

The scores, and what RYFT shows about food and nutrients, are information for you. A recovery estimate from a check-in rests on what you report and is not a measurement. RYFT makes no decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects. RYFT is not a medical device.

## 13. Changes

A changed policy gets a new version number, and the app asks for your consent again before the next upload.
